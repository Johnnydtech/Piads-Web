Once a venue owner realizes their screen can earn money from local advertisers, the next question is almost always the same: how much of the rotation should actually go to ads? Give up too little and you're leaving real revenue on the table. Give up too much and your own screen starts to feel like someone else's billboard — which annoys the customers you were trying to serve in the first place.

There's no single right answer, but there is a sensible range depending on your venue type, how long customers dwell, and what your screen is actually for. Here's how to think about it.

## The starting ratio, by venue type

| Venue type | Suggested ad share of rotation | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Cafes & coffee shops | 10–15% | Short dwell time; screen is mostly about menu and vibe |
| Gyms & fitness studios | 15–20% | Longer dwell time, repeat visitors tolerate more variety |
| Restaurants (counter-service) | 10–15% | Menu and specials need to dominate the loop |
| Salons & barbershops | 15–25% | Long appointment wait times make ads less intrusive |
| Waiting rooms (medical/dental) | 20–30% | Captive, often bored audience; ads double as a distraction |
| Retail stores | 10–15% | Screen should prioritize product and promotions |

These are starting points, not rules. A gym with a loyal membership base that checks in five days a week can tolerate fewer ads before it feels repetitive, since the same faces see the loop constantly. A waiting room with high turnover and a truly captive audience can run a higher ad share without anyone minding, because there's little else competing for attention.

## Why the ratio matters more than the absolute number of ads

Venue owners sometimes fixate on "how many ads" instead of "what percentage of the loop." A 60-second loop with one 8-second ad is roughly 13% ads. A 3-minute loop with the same single ad is under 5%. Keeping your total loop length reasonable — most venues do well with 60–120 seconds — means a fixed number of ad slots represents a meaningful chunk of a short loop, and a trivial one in a long, bloated loop that nobody watches all the way through anyway.

If your loop keeps growing because you never remove old content, your effective ad ratio silently shrinks even though you haven't added a single new ad. Prune your own content regularly, not just when you're deciding whether to add more advertising.

## Signs you've gone too far

A few warning signs that your ad share is too high for your venue:

- **Customers start commenting on it.** Even a joking "wow, a lot of ads today" from a regular is worth taking seriously.
- **Your own promotions get buried.** If a customer can't remember your current special because three ads played between menu slides, the ratio is working against you.
- **The loop feels long.** A screen that used to feel like ambient background now feels like something people are waiting to finish.

None of these mean advertising is a bad idea — they mean the ratio needs adjusting, not eliminating.

## Signs you're leaving money on the table

On the other end, a lot of venues set one ad slot and never revisit it for a year. Signs you could reasonably sell more inventory:

- Your screen sits mostly idle during slow hours with nothing but a static logo or clock
- You've had more advertiser interest than you have slots to offer
- Your own content loop is short enough that even 20–25% ad share wouldn't feel crowded

## A reasonable starting process

1. Start low — 10–15% of your loop — for the first month, even if your venue type suggests you could go higher
2. Watch for customer reaction and whether your own content still gets noticed
3. Raise the ratio gradually if the screen still feels mostly like "your" screen
4. Use dayparting to run a higher ratio during slow hours and a lower one during peak times
5. Review it quarterly — foot traffic, dwell time, and advertiser interest all shift over a year

## FAQ

**Is there an ideal ad percentage that works for every venue?**
No. It depends heavily on dwell time and what your screen is primarily for. A waiting room can tolerate a much higher ad share than a counter-service restaurant where the menu needs to dominate.

**Can I change my ad ratio later?**
Yes — there's no lock-in. Adjust it as often as you like based on customer feedback and how much advertiser interest you're seeing.

**Do I have to accept every ad that applies for a slot?**
No. Every ad requires your approval before it airs, regardless of how many slots you've opened up, so raising your ad ratio doesn't mean losing control over content.

**What if I don't want any ads at all?**
That's fine — accepting ads is optional. Many venues run PiAds purely for their own content and never open a slot to the marketplace.

**Does a higher ad ratio mean more work for me?**
Not really. Approving an ad takes a minute, and you set your ratio by how many slots you build into your own playlist — the marketplace fills them, you don't have to chase advertisers down.
