Most digital signage advice is written for chains running dozens of stores with an IT department behind them. That advice doesn't map well to a hardware store, a gift shop, a shoe store, or a sporting goods shop running one to five screens with an owner who also works the register.

At that scale, the questions are different: How many screens do I actually need? What do I put on them that isn't just "SALE" on a loop? Is this worth the hardware cost? Here's a practical answer for small retailers, not enterprise chains.

## How many screens does a small retail store need?

| Store size | Recommended screens | Typical placement |
|---|---|---|
| Under 1,000 sq ft | 1 | Near entrance or register |
| 1,000–3,000 sq ft | 2 | Entrance + register, or entrance + a category zone |
| 3,000–6,000 sq ft | 3–4 | Entrance, register, one per major department |
| 6,000+ sq ft | 4–5 | Entrance, register, department zones, fitting/consult area |

The honest trade-off: one well-placed screen near your register beats three screens scattered around a small store that customers only glance at from a distance. Don't buy screens to fill wall space — buy them to answer a specific customer question ("what's new," "what's on sale," "where's the checkout line moving").

## What to actually show

A small retailer's content mix should rotate through a short loop — 60–90 seconds is plenty for most stores:

- **New arrivals** — what came in this week, with a photo and shelf/aisle location
- **Current promotions** — clearer and more flexible than a paper sign taped to the window
- **Best sellers or staff picks** — social proof nudges undecided browsers
- **Loyalty program or email signup** — a QR code customers can scan while waiting
- **Store hours and upcoming events** — sidewalk sales, seasonal hours, local pop-ups
- **A local advertiser slide** — filling downtime with revenue instead of dead air

That last one matters more than it sounds. A screen sitting near your register is prime local advertising real estate during slow hours, and it doesn't cost you anything to fill it.

## What to skip until you actually scale past 5 screens

Small retailers often over-buy for a size of business they don't have yet. Skip these until you're running signage across multiple locations or a much larger floor:

- **Enterprise CMS platforms** with per-seat licensing and long contracts — you don't need multi-location role management for one storefront
- **Commercial-grade displays** — a consumer TV running 10–12 hours a day easily lasts 3–5 years in a retail setting; save the commercial panel budget for later
- **Custom content design services** — Canva templates and phone photography cover a small retailer's needs fine; see our [Canva-to-TV workflow](/blog/canva-digital-signage-display-on-tv) if you want a five-minute path from design to screen
- **Dedicated signage staff** — one person spending 15 minutes a week updating playlists is enough at this scale

## Getting started

1. Start with one screen near your highest-traffic point — usually the register or entrance
2. Build a short loop: new arrivals, one promotion, one loyalty or social CTA
3. Add a second screen only once the first is earning its keep
4. Open up a slot or two to local advertisers once you're comfortable with the content mix

## FAQ

**Do I need more than one screen to start?**
No. Most small retailers should start with a single screen at the highest-traffic point and expand only once it's clearly working.

**What's the cheapest way to test digital signage before buying hardware?**
A browser-based player on a spare laptop costs nothing and lets you preview content before committing to a Fire TV Stick.

**Can I run different content on each screen?**
Yes — each screen gets its own playlist, so your entrance screen can show promotions while your register screen shows loyalty and checkout-adjacent content.

**How much does digital signage cost for a small retail store overall?**
