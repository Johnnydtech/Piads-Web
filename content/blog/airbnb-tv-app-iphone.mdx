Hosts search for an "Airbnb TV app for iPhone" expecting to find something that puts house information on the rental's TV from their phone.

The good news is that the workflow you are imagining exists. The part that surprises people is which device does which job.

> PiAds is not affiliated with, endorsed by, or sponsored by Airbnb or Apple.

## The Short Version

**Your iPhone is the remote, not the screen.**

- The **TV** shows the welcome screen, driven by a cheap streaming stick plugged into it
- Your **iPhone** is how you set it up, change the guest name, and update the wifi or recommendations, from anywhere

Airbnb's own iPhone app does not display anything on a TV, and there is no Airbnb TV app on any platform. What hosts want is digital signage software, and the phone half of that is genuinely useful.

## Why Not Just Use the iPhone as the Screen?

You can technically mirror an iPhone or iPad to a TV with AirPlay, and some hosts try it. It falls apart quickly:

- Mirroring **stops when the device sleeps**, gets a call, or drops off wifi
- It **ties up a device permanently**, so it is an old iPad taped behind a TV
- There is **no automatic recovery** after a power cut, which is exactly when you are not there
- It cannot **greet a specific guest** without you doing it manually each time

A $30 streaming stick solves all four, because it boots straight into the content and reconnects on its own.

## What the iPhone Is Genuinely Good For

This is the workflow that makes a welcome screen practical across turnovers:

### Changing the guest name between stays

The highest-impact detail on any welcome screen is the guest's own name. It takes about ten seconds from your phone, and it is the difference between a screen that feels considered and one that feels like signage.

### Updating the wifi password

The moment you change the router password, you change it in one place and every screen in that property has it. No trip, no reprinted card, no 11pm text.

### Fixing a recommendation that closed

Local places close. A screen recommending a restaurant that shut two months ago is worse than no recommendation. Editing it from your phone while you remember is the only version of this that actually happens.

### Checking that a screen is alive

You can see whether the TV is online before a guest arrives, which is a much better way to find out about a dead stick than a message from the guest.

## What You Actually Need in the Room

Not an iPhone. The setup is:

| Piece | What it does | Cost |
|---|---|---|
| The TV already there | Displays | $0 |
| Fire TV Stick or Android box | Runs the welcome screen | ~$30 |
| Signage software | What you edit from your phone | ~$10/mo ad-free |
| Your iPhone | Remote control for all of it | $0 |

One important detail: plug the stick into a **wall outlet**, not the TV's USB port. On TV power it shuts down with the TV and misses your updates until the next guest turns it on.

## What About Apple TV?

If you already have an Apple TV in the rental, it is a more capable box than a streaming stick and can run signage. It is also five to six times the price of a Fire TV Stick, so it is rarely the right thing to buy specifically for this.

Use it if it is already on the wall. Buy a Fire TV Stick if it is not.

## The Honest Limitation

None of this lets you push something to the TV instantly on demand the way casting does. The screen checks for changes every few minutes, so an edit lands within a few minutes rather than the same second.

For a welcome screen that is the correct trade. It means the TV keeps working when the wifi drops, because it plays from content it already downloaded rather than streaming from your phone.

## FAQ

### Is there an Airbnb TV app for iPhone?

No. Airbnb's iPhone app manages bookings, not TVs. For hosts, the iPhone is how you control a welcome screen that a streaming stick displays.

### Can I use an iPhone or iPad as the TV screen in my rental?

Not reliably. AirPlay mirroring stops on sleep, calls or wifi changes and cannot recover from a power cut on its own.

### Can I update my Airbnb welcome screen from my phone?

Yes. Guest name, wifi, checkout time and recommendations all change from your phone and reach the TV within a few minutes.
