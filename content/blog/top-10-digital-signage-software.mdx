Search for digital signage software and you will find a hundred vendors who all describe themselves the same way. This is a straight comparison of the ten platforms that actually matter in 2026, what each is genuinely good at, and where each one falls down.

No platform here is bad. They are built for different buyers, and most of the frustration people have with signage software comes from picking one built for someone else.

## 1. Yodeck

The value leader, and the default recommendation for anyone running more than a handful of screens on a budget. Ships Raspberry Pi players, has a genuinely usable free single-screen tier, and the per-screen price drops sharply with volume.

**Best for:** Multi-screen small business, franchises watching cost per screen.
**Weakness:** The interface shows its age, and heavy customization gets fiddly.

## 2. OptiSigns

The broadest app library of any platform here. If you need a specific data source on screen, Google Slides, social feeds, dashboards, weather, queue systems, OptiSigns probably already has it.

**Best for:** Teams that need many third-party integrations.
**Weakness:** Per-screen cost climbs once you add the higher-tier features.

## 3. ScreenCloud

The enterprise standard. Strong permissions, multi-site organization, solid support, good uptime. It is built for a marketing team managing screens across many locations.

**Best for:** Larger organizations with a dedicated owner for signage.
**Weakness:** Priced well above what a single cafe or salon should pay.

## 4. Rise Vision

Free for schools, and genuinely good at it. A huge template library aimed at classrooms, cafeterias, and announcement boards, with an education-focused feature set.

**Best for:** K-12 and higher education.
**Weakness:** Commercial pricing is less compelling than the education offer.

## 5. Xibo

The most capable open-source option. Self-host it and you control everything, with no per-screen fee and no vendor between you and your data.

**Best for:** Organizations with IT staff and a policy against cloud tools.
**Weakness:** You are the support team. Budget real hours for setup and upkeep.

## 6. Screenly

The Raspberry Pi specialist. If your plan is a rack of Pis running kiosk displays, Screenly is purpose-built for that and has an open-source edition to start from.

**Best for:** Digital signage Raspberry Pi deployments, developer-run setups.
**Weakness:** Narrower feature set outside the Pi world.

## 7. NoviSign

The strongest interactive and touch support in this group, plus solid built-in widgets for polls, forms, and social walls.

**Best for:** Touchscreen kiosks, wayfinding, interactive retail.
**Weakness:** More platform than a passive menu board needs.

## 8. Look (formerly Look DS)

Simple, quick, and priced in the middle. Good template library for retail promotions without a long learning curve.

**Best for:** Retail shops that want a clean promotional loop fast.
**Weakness:** Fewer advanced scheduling and data features.

## 9. Fugo

Built with security review in mind, which matters if your IT department has to approve the tool. Clean modern interface and good remote device management.

**Best for:** Companies whose procurement asks hard security questions.
**Weakness:** Enterprise pricing for an enterprise process.

## 10. PiAds

The one platform here that treats a screen as a revenue line rather than a cost line. It does the normal signage job, playlists, scheduling, remote updates on Fire TV, Android TV, Raspberry Pi, Apple TV or any browser, and adds a local advertising marketplace on top.

Screens that enable approved ad slots are **free**, and the venue keeps **70% of cleared ad revenue**. Screens that stay entirely ad-free are **$10 per screen per month or $100 per year**. Every ad still needs venue approval, so you never get a competitor on your own wall.

**Best for:** Local venues with foot traffic, cafes, salons, gyms, bars, clinics, short-term rentals.
**Weakness:** The ad marketplace is strongest in markets where local advertiser demand already exists, and it does not run on Roku.

## How To Actually Choose

Ignore feature checklists. Three questions decide it:

**1. How many screens, and what is your ceiling per screen per month?** Under $10 a screen, look at Yodeck, OptiSigns or PiAds. Above that, you are paying for team features you may not need.

**2. What hardware are you running?** Most platforms support Fire TV, Android and Pi. Fewer do Apple TV well. None of them run natively on Roku, which is a closed platform, so if your TVs are Roku you will be adding a stick on another input.

**3. Could your screen carry advertising?** If you have real foot traffic and no policy against local ads, an ad-supported model can flip signage from a monthly bill into a monthly deposit. If your brand cannot host ads, pay the flat fee and skip the marketplace entirely.

## FAQ

### What are the top 10 digital signage software?

Yodeck, OptiSigns, ScreenCloud, Rise Vision, Xibo, Screenly, NoviSign, Look, Fugo and PiAds. They differ mainly in who they are priced for, not in whether they can play a loop on a TV.

### What is the best digital signage software for a small business?

Yodeck, OptiSigns or PiAds. All three run on cheap hardware and get a screen live in minutes. PiAds is the one that can make the screen free or profitable if you allow approved local ads.

### Is there a free digital signage app?

Yes. Yodeck offers one free screen, Xibo is free if you self-host, and PiAds is free for partner screens that enable ad slots.
