Pickleball is the fastest-growing sport in the country, and the venues built around it — indoor clubs, recreation centers with dedicated courts, private facilities — are discovering something interesting: their screens are just as underutilized as everyone else's.

Walk into any pickleball club today and you'll see TVs in the lobby showing cable sports, maybe a whiteboard with open court hours, and a bulletin board near the door with flyers for upcoming tournaments. Meanwhile, dozens of players are sitting in the waiting area, hanging out between matches, and watching the courts from the sideline.

That's a captive, high-value audience with money to spend and time to spend it. Here's how forward-thinking pickleball clubs are using digital signage to improve the player experience and build a new revenue stream at the same time.

## Why Pickleball Clubs Are a Natural Fit for Digital Signage

Pickleball venues have a combination of traits that makes them unusually effective for digital signage.

**Extended wait and dwell time.** Players book courts in advance but arrive early. They hang out between matches. They socialize after games. Most serious players spend 2-3 hours at the club even if they're only on the court for 90 minutes. That's 30-60 minutes of genuine waiting time — and all of it is screen-facing time if your content is worth looking at.

**Social, group-oriented environment.** Pickleball is inherently social. Players come with partners, join leagues, make friends at open play. They're in a great mood, they're talking to each other, and they're discussing plans — where to eat after, what gear to buy next, what they're doing this weekend. That's a mindset advertisers pay a premium to reach.

**High-income, active demographic.** Pickleball's fastest-growing segment is the 35-65 bracket — established adults with disposable income who are active, health-conscious, and locally engaged. This is one of the most valuable consumer demographics in any community. Local businesses line up to reach them.

**Repeat visitation.** League players show up multiple times per week. Open play regulars come 3-4 times a week. This means high frequency: your content — and the ads running alongside it — reach the same people over and over. Advertisers love frequency.

## 6 Ways Pickleball Clubs Use Digital Signage

### 1. Live Court Status and Queue Boards

The most immediately useful thing a digital screen can do at a pickleball club is show who's on which court, what's coming up next, and how long the wait is for open play.

Clubs that still manage this with a clipboard or a whiteboard cause unnecessary friction. Players hover near the check-in desk, interrupt staff, or miss their court call because they weren't paying attention. A clear, prominently placed court status board fixes all of this.

Display:
- Current court assignments with player names or group numbers
- Estimated time remaining per court
- Next-up queue for open play
- Court availability for same-day bookings

This single screen change often reduces check-in desk interruptions by 60-80%. Staff can focus on service instead of answering "how long's the wait?"

### 2. Clinic and Program Schedules

Most pickleball clubs run a constant stream of programming: beginner clinics, intermediate drills, advanced strategy sessions, junior programs, cardio pickleball, round robins, ladder leagues, and open play events.

The problem is that players don't always know what's available. They show up for open play, not realizing a beginner clinic is running in the next time slot — exactly the class their spouse keeps saying they should take.

Digital screens can surface this programming in context: while someone is waiting for their court, show them what's coming up this week. A well-placed schedule slide converts bystanders into program participants without any additional marketing effort.

Include:
- Weekly clinic schedule with skill levels
- Upcoming round robins and tournaments
- New program launches
- Registration links or QR codes

### 3. Tournament Brackets and Live Results

If your club runs leagues or tournaments — and most established clubs do — live bracket updates on a screen are transformational. Instead of everyone crowding around a printed bracket sheet or refreshing an app, a screen in the viewing area shows live standings, current match results, and upcoming matchups.

This keeps spectators engaged and gives your facility the energy of a real sporting event. Players who might have left after their match stick around to watch. The vibe goes up. Dwell time increases. And more time in your club means more opportunities to sell concessions, merchandise, court time, or clinics.

### 4. Pro Tips and Game Improvement Content

Your most engaged players are always looking to get better. Short form instructional content — a third-shot drop technique tip, a common beginner mistake and how to fix it, a defense pattern for doubles — is exactly what a waiting player wants to see.

This content builds loyalty. Players start to associate your club with quality instruction, not just court access. When they're choosing between your facility and the one that opened across town, they remember that yours feels like it cares about their game.

Content ideas:
- 30-60 second drill demonstrations
- "Tip of the week" from one of your instructors
- Paddle maintenance and gear care
- Warm-up and injury prevention routines
- Rules reminders (kitchen violations, service rules)

### 5. Merchandise and Concession Upsells

Pickleball is a gear-hungry sport. Players go through paddles, shoes, and balls constantly. They buy apparel, grips, and accessories. If your club sells any of these — in a pro shop or even just from a small display shelf — your screens should be promoting them.

A screen near the waiting area with a well-shot photo of a new paddle model, with price and a note about in-club availability, drives more sales than a paddle sitting quietly in a rack. People didn't know to look for it. Screens make them look.

Same for any concessions or food and drink you offer. A cold drink promotion during a summer league night at the right moment converts reliably. Make it visible.

- **Sports massage and chiropractic** — High overlap with the active 40-60 demographic.
- **Health food restaurants and cafes** — Players are hungry after 90 minutes of play. Where they go next is an open question.
- **Athletic wear and sporting goods** — Local retailers, especially those carrying pickleball-specific gear, want your players to know they exist.
- **Fitness and wellness studios** — Cross-training athletes (your players) are exactly who yoga studios and strength gyms recruit.

## A Note on Content Strategy

The clubs that get the most from their screens treat them like a member service, not a billboard. The content rotation that works best puts your players first: court status, schedule, useful tips, community news. Ads are woven in, not plastered over everything.

Keep the ad frequency at or below 30% of screen time. Players will accept — and even appreciate — well-targeted local ads when the surrounding content is genuinely useful. They won't accept a screen that's mostly ads with token club content squeezed in.

A good 90-second waiting area loop might look like:

1. Court status / queue board (20 seconds)
2. Upcoming clinic or program (15 seconds)
3. Local advertiser ad (15 seconds)
4. Game tip or technique content (15 seconds)
5. Tournament or league standings (15 seconds)
6. Merchandise or concession highlight (10 seconds)

That's a useful, watchable loop — and a local business is paying you to be part of it.
