Yodeck shows up in almost every "best digital signage software" list, and for good reason — it's mature, it has a huge app library, and its free single-screen plan is a genuinely generous way to try signage for the first time. If you're a local venue weighing whether to stick with it or look elsewhere, the honest answer is: it depends on what you're optimizing for. This post walks through where Yodeck holds up, where it starts to feel like overhead for a one-to-five-screen local business, and what actually changes if you move to something built around hardware you probably already own.

## Where Yodeck genuinely holds up

Credit where it's due. Yodeck has been around long enough to prove itself at scale, and its app library — weather widgets, social feeds, dashboards, calendars — covers use cases a smaller, more focused platform like PiAds doesn't try to match. If you're already running a healthy fleet of Pi players across several locations, have IT staff who don't mind imaging SD cards, and don't care about turning screen time into ad revenue, there's a real case for staying put. Ripping out a working setup to save a modest amount per screen isn't automatically the right call.

Where the calculus shifts is for a smaller, single-site operation — a [cafe](/digital-signage-for/cafes-coffee-shops), a [gym](/digital-signage-for/gyms-fitness-studios), a [salon](/digital-signage-for/salons-barbershops) — where there's no dedicated IT person, and the "signage admin" is also the person running the counter.

## The Raspberry Pi tax

Yodeck's Pi-first design is its biggest strength and its biggest friction point, depending on who's asking. A Raspberry Pi signage player means:

- Sourcing the board, a case, a power supply, and an SD card separately (or buying a kit)
- Imaging the SD card with Yodeck's OS before the device does anything
- Re-flashing or replacing the card when it eventually wears out — SD cards are the most common point of failure in always-on Pi deployments
- No native option for the Fire TV Stick that's probably already plugged into the TV behind the counter

None of this is a dealbreaker for a team that manages hardware for a living. For a single-location owner-operator, it's an extra errand layered onto every new screen, and a recurring one if a card fails eighteen months in.

## FAQ

**Is Yodeck free?**
Yodeck offers a free plan limited to one screen with Yodeck branding. Beyond that single screen, or if you want the branding removed, you move to a paid tier.

**Do I need a Raspberry Pi to use Yodeck?**
Raspberry Pi is Yodeck's primary supported hardware path, and most of its documentation and setup flow assumes it. That's the main thing that changes if you switch to a Fire TV-first platform — there's no SD card to image.

**Will I lose my existing playlists and schedules if I switch?**
No — your media re-uploads directly, and playlists/schedules get rebuilt in the new platform's editor. It's manual re-entry, not a transfer, so budget some time for it, but it's not technically complicated.

**Does PiAds support the same hardware Yodeck does?**
PiAds runs natively on Fire TV (Stick, Cube, Fire TV televisions, Vega OS), Android, and Raspberry Pi, plus a browser-based web player with no hardware at all. If your current Pi hardware is healthy, you can keep using it — you're not forced to buy a Fire TV Stick to switch.

**Is switching worth it just to save money?**
Only you can weigh that against the time cost of re-setting up playlists and schedules. Where it tends to be worth it is when the Pi maintenance itself (SD card failures, imaging new devices) has become its own recurring chore — at that point, the switch pays for itself in avoided hassle, not just subscription price.
