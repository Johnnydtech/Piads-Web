Thanksgiving week is when most small businesses realize their screen is still showing the same October special. The holiday season — Black Friday through New Year's — is the single highest-traffic, highest-spend stretch of the year for retail, restaurants, salons, and gyms alike. A screen that's still running summer content isn't just a missed opportunity; it actively signals that nothing in the building is current.

This is a working list of what to actually put on a venue screen between now and January 1, organized by what's driving customer behavior each week, not a generic "holiday content" checklist.

## What to run, and when

| Window | What's happening | Screen content that works |
|---|---|---|
| Nov 20–26 | Pre-Thanksgiving planning | Hours/closures, gift card teaser, "order ahead for the holiday" |
| Nov 27–30 (Black Friday weekend) | Peak retail urgency | Live countdown, doorbuster specials, Small Business Saturday callout |
| Dec 1–14 | Gift-buying window | Gift guide slides, bundle/gift-card promos, "X days left to ship" |
| Dec 15–24 | Last-minute rush | Extended hours, last-call-for-shipping cutoffs, walk-in gift cards |
| Dec 26–31 | Post-holiday lull | Clearance/returns policy, New Year's promos, gift-card redemption reminder |

Each window calls for a different screen, not just a different slide tacked onto the existing loop.

## 1. A real countdown, not a static "Black Friday" graphic

A ticking clock to "Doors close early at 6pm" or "Sale ends in 4:12:33" reads completely differently than a banner that just says "Black Friday Sale." The clock creates urgency a static image can't, and it works for Small Business Saturday (November 28 this year) just as well as for a flash discount on December 23. If you've used a countdown for happy hour before, this is the exact same mechanic pointed at a bigger moment — see our [content scheduling guide](/blog/content-scheduling-for-venue-screens) for how to daypart it automatically so it's only live during the actual sale window.

## 2. Gift cards, front and center

Gift cards are the highest-margin thing most local businesses sell, and the screen is free real estate to push them. A simple slide — "Gift cards. Any amount. No expiration." — with a QR code to buy online outperforms a printed table-tent because it's visible from across the room, not just to someone already at the counter. Salons, restaurants, and gyms should be running this slide from Black Friday weekend straight through December 24; retail stores should keep it live into late December as a last-minute, no-shipping-required option.

## 3. Small Business Saturday messaging that's actually specific

"Shop small" on its own has gone generic. What works better: naming the specific reason to choose this venue over a big-box alternative that day — a one-day bundle, a free gift with purchase over a threshold, or simply "Every dollar spent here stays in Arlington." [Retail stores](/digital-signage-for/retail-stores) running one screen or five should treat November 28 as its own dedicated content day, not a sub-slide inside the regular Black Friday loop.

## 4. Shipping and pickup cutoff dates

Nothing frustrates a December shopper more than buying something that won't arrive in time. If you ship product or take special orders, a simple "Order by Dec 18 for guaranteed delivery" slide — updated as the date approaches — answers the question before anyone has to ask the counter staff. [Restaurants](/digital-signage-for/restaurants) taking holiday catering orders should run the same logic: a visible cutoff date for Christmas Eve or New Year's Eve orders.

## 5. Extended or adjusted hours, loudly

Holiday hours change constantly and customers rarely check a website before walking up to a locked door. A screen visible from the sidewalk or lobby — hours for Thanksgiving Day, Christmas Eve, Christmas Day, and New Year's Eve, updated as soon as they're finalized — prevents the single worst holiday customer experience: showing up to a closed business with no notice.

## 6. Seasonal menu, service, or product spotlights

Peppermint lattes, holiday-scented products, limited seasonal services (a gym's "12 Days of Fitness" class pass, a salon's holiday party blowout package) all deserve their own slide, separate from the regular offering. Limited-time framing — "back next December" — creates urgency that a permanent menu item never will.

## 7. Staff holiday picks and gift guides

A slide with a staff photo and a genuine recommendation — "Maria's pick for the person who has everything" — works the same way it does the rest of the year, just pointed at gift-giving instead of a daily special. It's low-effort content (a phone photo, two sentences) that fills loop time without requiring new design work every week.

## 9. New Year's transition content

By December 26, the holiday framing should flip. Clearance and return-policy slides, gift-card-redemption reminders ("still have a gift card? Here's what's new"), and January-preview content (gym sign-up pushes, "new year" promos) should be queued up in advance so nobody has to remember to swap the loop on December 26 itself.

## Honest trade-off: don't over-build this

A single-screen cafe or salon doesn't need all nine of these running at once. Three or four — a countdown during peak sale windows, the gift-card slide, hours, and one seasonal spotlight — covers most of what holiday traffic actually responds to. Adding every idea on this list to one loop just means each individual slide gets seen less often; a focused four-slide holiday loop beats a bloated twelve-slide one. Save the rest for next year or for a second screen if you have one.

## FAQ

**How far in advance should holiday content go live?**
Gift card and seasonal spotlight content can go live now. Black Friday and Small Business Saturday countdowns should be scheduled to appear 3–5 days before, not on the day itself — most shoppers plan ahead.

**Do I need different content for each day of the Black Friday weekend?**
Not entirely different, but Small Business Saturday specifically deserves its own slide rather than being folded into a generic "Black Friday weekend" message — it's a distinct shopping occasion with its own audience intent.

**What if I don't have anything special to offer for the holidays?**
Gift cards, hours, and a staff pick are enough. You don't need a discount to justify holiday content — visibility and clear information (hours, cutoffs) matter as much as promotions.

**Can I run holiday ads from other local businesses even in December?**
Yes — if anything, December ad demand from local businesses is higher, since everyone is pushing gift cards and seasonal specials at once. Every ad still requires your approval before it airs.
