You've got a TV, $30 to spend, and a "TVs I own" drawer full of streaming sticks. Before you buy anything new, the obvious question: can the Chromecast already in that drawer run your digital signage? Short answer — not well, and here's why, plus what to buy instead.

If you only remember one line from this post: **Chromecast is a casting receiver, not a signage player, and that difference is the whole story.**

## Why Chromecast falls short for signage

A Chromecast doesn't run apps on its own. It waits for another device — your phone, a laptop, a Chrome browser tab — to "cast" a stream to it. That's brilliant for watching a movie you started on your phone. It's a poor fit for a screen that's supposed to run itself for weeks:

- **No native app.** There's no PiAds (or any signage) app installed on the device itself. Someone has to keep a source casting to it.
- **The source has to stay on.** If the casting laptop sleeps, closes its browser tab, or restarts, your screen goes blank — and nobody notices until a customer does.
- **WiFi hiccups break the cast.** A brief dropout doesn't just pause playback; it can kill the cast session entirely, and it won't resume itself.
- **No offline caching.** Native players keep showing your last-loaded content through a short outage. A Chromecast has nothing to fall back on — it's just a receiver.
- **No health monitoring.** Because there's no signage app running on it, a Chromecast can't report online/offline status or last-seen to a dashboard the way a real player does.

None of that matters for a conference-room screen you cast a slide deck to for an hour. It matters a lot for a screen that's supposed to run your menu, promotions, and ads all day, every day, with nobody standing by to restart it.

## Where a Chromecast is genuinely fine

To be fair to it: Chromecast is a good $20 device for what it's built for.

- Casting a one-time presentation or slideshow to a screen for a meeting
- A temporary demo you're actively driving from a laptop
- A screen you're personally standing next to and can recast in ten seconds if something drops

If that's your actual use case, don't spend more — a Chromecast (or just AirPlay/casting from whatever you have) is enough, and there's no reason to buy signage software for a one-hour cast.

## FAQ

**Can I use a Chromecast with PiAds?**
Not directly — PiAds doesn't publish a Chromecast receiver app, and for the reliability reasons above, we'd steer you toward a Fire TV Stick instead. It's a similar price and takes about the same five minutes to set up.

**Is Fire TV Stick actually more reliable than Chromecast for this?**
Yes, structurally so — it runs the app itself instead of depending on another device to keep casting to it, so it survives WiFi blips, power cuts, and someone closing a laptop lid.

**What if I already own several Chromecasts — do I have to throw them out?**
No, but repurpose them for what they're good at (casting a laptop screen in a meeting) rather than unattended venue signage. For the TV that needs to run your promotions all day, a $30 Fire TV Stick will save you more in avoided headaches than it costs.

**Do I need a different signage plan for Fire TV vs. a Raspberry Pi?**
No — one PiAds plan works across every supported device, all at $10 per screen per month, so you can mix Fire TV Sticks and Pis across a venue and manage them from the same dashboard.

**What's the cheapest reliable option if I have zero budget for hardware?**
A browser-based web player running on a spare laptop or the TV's built-in browser costs nothing beyond the software, though it's less robust than a native app for a screen that has to survive weeks unattended.
