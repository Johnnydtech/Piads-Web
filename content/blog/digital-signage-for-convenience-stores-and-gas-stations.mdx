A convenience store or gas station sees more strangers walk through the door in a single day than most restaurants see all week. Most of that traffic gets a static price sign, a taped-up flyer, or nothing at all. That's a lot of eyeballs going to waste on customers who are already standing still, waiting to pay.

Digital signage fits this environment well for one specific reason: dwell time is short but volume is huge. A customer at the counter looks at a screen for 10-30 seconds. A customer waiting for a pump or paying inside might glance at one for even less. You don't need long-form content — you need something that lands fast, on a lot of people, all day long.

## Where screens actually earn their keep

Not every corner of a convenience store benefits equally from a screen. Here's where they perform, and where they're a waste of a TV.

| Location | Dwell time | Best content | Worth it? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Checkout counter | 15-45 sec | Promotions, loyalty app QR code, local ads | Yes — highest-traffic spot in the store |
| Coffee/fountain station | 20-60 sec | Drink promos, food combos, local ads | Yes — customers are standing and looking for something to do |
| Cooler aisle / walk-in door | 5-15 sec | Simple price or promo callouts only | Marginal — too brief for anything but one clear message |
| Forecourt/pump-top | 60-180 sec (fueling) | Fuel rewards, food combos, local ads, weather | Yes, if your pumps support a screen — long, captive dwell time |
| Front window facing the lot | Passer-by, seconds | Big, simple branding only | Rarely worth dedicated signage software |

The honest trade-off: if your store is small enough that a customer can see the entire counter and cooler in one glance, one screen at the register beats two or three screens scattered around. A second or third screen only pays off once you have distinct zones — pumps, a food-service counter, and a register — each with its own audience and its own moment.

## What to put on the screen

Content needs to work in seconds, not minutes. A 45-60 second loop is usually enough:

- **Food and drink combos** — a hot dog + fountain drink bundle, a coffee + pastry deal, anything that nudges a $3 stop toward a $7 one
- **Loyalty or rewards program** — a QR code customers can scan while the register catches up
- **Fuel rewards or point multipliers** — especially effective on forecourt screens, where the customer is standing there for the length of a fill-up
- **Local advertiser slides** — a nearby car wash, auto shop, or fast-casual restaurant reaching drivers who are already out and about
- **Weather and local alerts** — genuinely useful content that keeps people glancing back at the screen instead of tuning it out
- **New product callouts** — energy drinks, seasonal snacks, anything you want to push before it sits on the shelf

Keep slide duration short — 6-10 seconds per slide is plenty at a checkout counter where the average interaction barely lasts half a minute. On a forecourt screen where someone's fueling for two or three minutes, you can afford slightly longer slides or a bit more variety in the loop. ## Hardware: match it to the environment

This is where convenience stores differ from a typical retail shop or cafe. Screens near coolers deal with cold and condensation; screens near windows and forecourts deal with direct sun and heat; screens indoors are a much easier environment.

- **Indoor counter and coffee-station screens** — a standard TV with a [Fire TV Stick](/blog/fire-tv-stick-digital-signage-setup-guide) is plenty. It's cheap, boots straight into your content, and needs no IT visit. See the [Fire TV player page](/players/fire-tv) for the full lineup — Stick, Cube, Fire TV televisions, and the newer Vega OS devices are all supported.
- **Forecourt or pump-top screens** — if your pumps already have screens, check whether they accept a standard signage feed; if you're adding a display near the pumps yourself, look for a sunlight-readable, weatherproof enclosure rather than a consumer TV, which will wash out and overheat outdoors.
- **Testing before committing** — if you're not sure a location is worth a dedicated player yet, a browser-based web player on a spare tablet or laptop costs nothing extra and lets you preview content before buying hardware. Compare all the options on our [device page](/devices).

## Getting started

1. Start with one screen at the register — it's the highest-traffic, highest-dwell-time spot in almost every store
2. Build a short loop: one combo promo, one loyalty CTA, one local ad slot
3. Add a coffee-station or forecourt screen once the first is clearly earning attention
4. Open a slot or two to nearby local advertisers once you're comfortable with your own content mix

## FAQ

**Do I need a screen at every pump?**
No. Start with one high-traffic indoor screen — usually the register — before investing in forecourt or pump-top displays, which typically need weatherproof hardware and cost more to install.

**What's the best content for a 20-second glance?**
One idea per slide, in large text: a combo deal, a loyalty QR code, or a single local ad. Dense pricing grids or long paragraphs get skipped in a checkout line.

**Can I run ads from local businesses on my screens?**
Yes. You approve every ad before it airs, and you keep 75% of the revenue from every booking — the rest of your content stays entirely under your control.

**Will a consumer TV survive near a walk-in cooler or a sunny front window?**
Near a cooler, generally fine — just watch for condensation. Near direct sun or outdoors at a pump, no — use a sunlight-readable, weatherproof display instead of a standard consumer TV.
