A grocery store or independent market has something most venues would kill for: repeat weekly visits from the same households, long dwell times at the deli and checkout line, and a captive audience that's already thinking about spending money. Most of that attention still goes to a laminated sign taped to a shelf edge or a paper flyer by the door.

Digital signage fits grocery retail better than almost any other venue type, for a simple reason: shoppers spend 20-40 minutes in the store, and several of those minutes are spent standing still — waiting in a checkout line, waiting for a deli order, or scanning a shelf for a price. Those are exactly the moments a screen can fill with something more useful than silence.

## Where screens actually earn their keep

Not every part of a grocery store benefits equally from a screen. Here's where they perform, and where they're a waste of a TV.

| Location | Dwell time | Best content | Worth it? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Checkout lanes | 1-5 min (line-dependent) | Weekly specials, loyalty app signup, local ads | Yes — the longest guaranteed dwell time in the store |
| Deli / bakery counter | 2-8 min (order wait) | Menu boards, today's specials, local ads | Yes — captive audience with nothing else to look at |
| Endcap / aisle entry displays | 5-15 sec | Single promo callout only | Marginal — too brief for rotating content |
| Entrance / front-of-store | Passer-by, seconds | Store hours, weekly circular highlights | Limited — fine as a secondary screen, not a first one |
| Customer service / pharmacy wait area | 3-10 min | Local ads, community notices, health tips | Yes, if you have a pharmacy or service counter |

The honest trade-off: a single-aisle corner market doesn't need five screens any more than a convenience store does. One screen at checkout, done well, will outperform three screens scattered across the floor that nobody designed content for. Add a second screen at the deli or pharmacy counter only once the first is clearly holding attention — that's the same staged approach that works for [retail stores](/digital-signage-for/retail-stores) more broadly.

## What to put on the screen

Grocery shoppers are price- and deal-driven, so content should lean into that instinct rather than fight it:

- **Weekly circular highlights** — the five or six deals you most want to move, rotated instead of buried in a paper insert most shoppers never unfold
- **Deli and bakery specials** — today's soup, a fresh-baked item, a sandwich combo — content that changes daily and justifies checking the screen on repeat visits
- **Loyalty program signup** — a QR code at checkout, where shoppers are already standing with their phone out to pay
- **Local advertiser slides** — a nearby pharmacy, auto shop, restaurant, or gym reaching the same households that shop here weekly
- **Store-specific notices** — holiday hours, recall notices, or a new self-checkout lane opening — genuinely useful content shoppers will actually read
- **Seasonal and recipe tie-ins** — pairing a produce special with a quick recipe idea gives shoppers a reason to buy more than they planned

Keep individual slides to 8-12 seconds at checkout, where lines move unpredictably and you want every shopper to catch at least one full message. At the deli counter, where the wait is longer and more predictable, you can run a slower loop with more variety. The same density rules that apply to any price-heavy display are covered in our [menu board design guide](/blog/digital-menu-board-design-readable-from-10-feet) — readable from a few feet away, one idea per slide, no dense grids of small text.

## Getting started

1. Start with one screen at your busiest checkout lane — it has the longest guaranteed dwell time in the store
2. Build a short loop: one or two weekly specials, a loyalty signup, and a local ad slot
3. Add a deli or pharmacy counter screen once the first is clearly earning attention
4. Open a slot or two to nearby local advertisers once you're comfortable with your own content mix

## FAQ

**Do I need a screen at every checkout lane?**
No. Start with your busiest lane and the deli counter, where dwell time is longest. Expand to other lanes only once you have a content loop worth repeating.

**What's the best content for a line that moves at an unpredictable pace?**
Short, self-contained slides — one deal or one message each — so a shopper who only sees 10 seconds of the loop still gets a complete idea.

**Can I run ads from local businesses on my screens?**
Yes. You approve every ad before it airs, and you keep 75% of the revenue from every booking — your own content stays entirely under your control.

**Will a consumer TV hold up near coolers or a busy deli counter?**
Generally yes indoors, as long as it's not in direct sun or exposed to outdoor temperature swings. Standard TVs with a Fire TV Stick are the normal setup for checkout and deli-counter screens.
