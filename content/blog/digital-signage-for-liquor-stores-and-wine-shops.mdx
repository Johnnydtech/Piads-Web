Walk into most liquor stores and wine shops and the walls do almost none of the selling. Hundreds of bottles, a handful of hand-written shelf tags, and a customer standing in the wine aisle trying to remember whether they wanted a Malbec or a Tempranillo. That's a lot of inventory asking to be discovered — and a lot of missed chances to upsell, cross-sell, or just help someone find something they'll actually like.

A screen near the register or above the wine wall fixes a real problem: browsers don't know what to buy, and staff can't be everywhere at once. Done well, it also turns into a small, steady stream of ad revenue from businesses that want exactly your customer.

Here's how liquor stores and wine shops are putting screens to work.

## Why liquor stores are a good fit for digital signage

| Store trait | Why it matters for signage |
|---|---|
| High SKU count, low staff-to-customer ratio | A screen does the recommending when staff can't get to every aisle |
| Inventory turns over constantly (new arrivals, limited releases, closeouts) | Paper shelf tags can't keep up; screens update in seconds |
| Purchases are often occasion-driven (dinner party, gift, holiday) | Screens can prompt pairing and gifting ideas at the moment of decision |
| Regulars visit weekly or biweekly | Enough repeat exposure for a rotating loop of deals and events to actually land |
| Customers are 21+, often higher discretionary spenders | An attractive audience for restaurants, caterers, and event businesses |

None of that requires anything exotic — a single screen near the register or the featured-bottle end cap covers most of the value. The fundamentals are the same as [any small retail store running digital signage](/digital-signage-for/retail-stores) — inventory-driven content, high visibility at the point of decision — with a few category-specific twists covered below.

## 6 ways liquor stores and wine shops use their screens

### 1. Price drops and weekly deals

This is the highest-ROI use of the screen, and the easiest to set up. Rotate through:

- This week's price drops, with the discount called out clearly
- Mix-and-match case deals ("Any 6 bottles, 10% off")
- Closeout and limited-quantity bottles before they're gone

Update it once a week from your phone. A shelf tag gets ignored after the first visit; a screen that visibly changes keeps regulars checking it.

### 2. Tasting notes and pairing suggestions

Most customers in the wine or whiskey aisle aren't experts — they want a nudge. A screen near the featured section can do what a knowledgeable staffer would, at scale:

- "Pairs well with" suggestions tied to what's in the case (grilling season, holiday roasts, taco night)
- Simple tasting notes for a rotating "bottle of the week"
- A short "if you liked X, try Y" comparison for popular styles

This is where digital signage genuinely increases basket size — customers buy the second bottle when the pairing idea is put in front of them, not left to guess.

### 3. New arrivals and limited releases

Allocated bourbons, small-batch wines, seasonal beer releases — these sell themselves once people know they exist. A screen is a far more reliable notification system than a chalkboard by the door that only the first 20 customers see.

Show what just arrived, how limited the quantity is, and any purchase limits. Urgency, shown honestly, moves inventory faster than a silent shelf ever will.

### 4. Events: tastings, release nights, and classes

If you run in-store tastings, sommelier nights, or a monthly whiskey club, your screen is a built-in promotion channel that costs nothing per use — unlike social ads, which cost money every time you want to remind people.

- Upcoming tasting event dates, with a QR code to RSVP
- Recap photos from the last event, to build FOMO for the next one
- Loyalty or club program details for regulars

### 5. Responsible messaging and compliance

Alcohol retail comes with real regulatory guardrails, and it's worth being direct about the trade-off: this isn't a category where you can run whatever content you want. Age-verification reminders, responsible-drinking messaging, and any required disclaimers should have a permanent, unskippable slot in the loop — not get crowded out by promotional content. Check your state's specific rules on in-store alcohol advertising before running third-party ads; requirements vary more here than in almost any other retail category.

### 6. Local advertiser revenue

Your customer is a genuinely valuable audience for a specific set of local businesses — people who are actively planning a dinner, a party, or a gift, with money already in hand.

Good local-advertiser fits for a liquor store or wine shop:

- **Restaurants and caterers** — Complementary, not competitive; a restaurant's ad next to your wine wall is a natural fit.
- **Event and wedding venues** — Wine and spirits purchases spike around events; the audience overlap is strong.
- **Cheese shops and gourmet food stores** — The classic pairing business, and an easy visual tie-in with your tasting content.
- **Local breweries and distilleries** — Especially ones you don't carry, or that want to promote a taproom visit alongside your retail sales.
- **Gift and flower shops** — Same occasions (anniversaries, holidays, hostess gifts) driving both categories.

## Where to place screens

| Location | Best content | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Above the register | Price drops, deals, event promos | Guaranteed eye contact during checkout wait |
| Featured/end-cap display | Tasting notes, pairing suggestions | Right where the decision is being made |
| Front window (facing outward) | Hours, weekly deal, "new arrivals" teaser | Pulls foot traffic in from the sidewalk |
| Wine or spirits aisle | Category-specific pairing content | Helps browsers without needing staff |

Most single-location stores do well with one or two screens — the register and one featured-category spot cover most of the value before a third screen starts to matter.

## What you need to get started

**Per screen:**
- Your existing TV, or a new 32-43" display ($130-300) — liquor stores rarely need anything larger
- A media player: a native [Fire TV Stick](/players/fire-tv) ($30-50) is the simplest reliable option, though [any supported device](/devices) — Android, Raspberry Pi, or a zero-hardware browser player — works
- Your existing WiFi
- A PiAds account to manage content, scheduling, and ad approvals from one dashboard

**Content to create upfront:**
- 3-5 current deal/price-drop slides, updated weekly
- 2-3 tasting note or pairing slides tied to your featured section
- 1 responsible-drinking / age-verification slide, permanently scheduled
- 1 upcoming event slide with a QR code, if you run tastings or classes

Budget 30-45 minutes for your first screen. Most of that time goes into writing the deal and pairing copy, not the technical setup — which is the right place to spend it.

## Honest trade-offs

If you run a small shop with light foot traffic and a simple, stable inventory, a printed shelf-tag system might genuinely be enough — the case for a screen gets stronger as your SKU count, deal frequency, and event calendar grow. A screen also won't fix a confusing store layout or an under-staffed floor; it supplements good service, it doesn't replace it.

And because of the compliance angle above, don't treat this category the same as a cafe or gym when it comes to ad content — build in the required messaging first, then layer promotions and local ads around it.

## FAQ

**Can I run local ads for other alcohol brands or bars on my screen?**
Usually, but check your state's alcohol advertising rules first — some states restrict alcohol-related advertising in retail settings more tightly than general retail. Non-alcohol local advertisers (restaurants, caterers, event venues) are the safer, easier default. [How local businesses advertise on venue screens](/blog/how-to-advertise-on-local-venue-screens) covers how approval works.

**Do prices change too often to put on screen?**
That's exactly the problem digital signage solves — you can update a price-drop slide in under a minute from your phone, which is faster than reprinting a shelf tag. Just don't put every SKU's price on screen; feature a rotating handful of deals instead of trying to replace your shelf tags entirely.

**Do I need a big TV for a small shop?**
No — a 32-43" screen near the register or featured display is plenty for most single-location stores. Save the larger display for a front window or a wide-open tasting area, if you have one.
