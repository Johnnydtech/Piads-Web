Search "Airbnb TV app" and you will find a lot of confusion, because two completely different groups use that phrase.

**Guests** mean "how do I log into my Netflix on this TV." **Hosts** mean "how do I make the TV in my rental do something useful instead of showing a blue input screen."

This guide is for hosts. It covers what actually exists, what it costs, and what works on which device.

> PiAds is not affiliated with, endorsed by, or sponsored by Airbnb. "Airbnb" is used here only to describe the kind of property this software is used in.

## First, the Short Answer: There Is No Airbnb TV App

Airbnb publishes apps for iPhone, Android and the web. All of them are for finding, booking and managing stays. **Airbnb has never published a TV app**, for hosts or guests.

So if you are hunting for an official one, you can stop. What you actually want is a **guest welcome screen**, which is built with ordinary digital signage software running on a cheap streaming stick.

## What Hosts Actually Want the TV To Do

Talk to enough hosts and the wish list is remarkably consistent:

1. **Show the wifi name and password** so it is not a text message at 11pm
2. **Show checkout time and house rules** without a laminated sheet on the fridge
3. **Greet the guest by name** so the place feels considered rather than transactional
4. **Recommend local places** you actually like, not whatever the algorithm suggests
5. **Update remotely** so you are not driving over between turnovers
6. **Not break the TV** for guests who just want to watch something

That last one matters more than the rest combined, and it is where a lot of setups go wrong.

## The Options

### Option 1: A Digital Signage App (What Most Hosts End Up With)

Install a signage app on a streaming stick, build one screen with your details, and update it from your phone.

**What it costs:** About $30 for a Fire TV Stick if you do not already have one, plus software. With PiAds an ad-free screen is **$10 per screen per month, or $100 per year**.

**Why hosts pick it:** It does the whole list above, it runs on hardware you can buy at any supermarket, and you can change the guest's name and your recommendations from your phone between cleanings.

### Option 2: A Free Ad-Supported Option

Several signage platforms are free because they sell advertising on your screen. This is a genuinely good trade in a cafe or a gym.

In a rental it is a judgment call. Ads on the TV in someone's living room read differently from ads in a waiting room, and a guest paying $200 a night may not love it. Some hosts are fine with it, particularly if the ads are local restaurants and activities that double as recommendations. Many are not.

With PiAds the choice is yours per screen: a **partner screen** that carries approved local ads is free and pays you 70% of cleared bookings, or an **ad-free screen** is $10 a month. For most rentals, hosts choose ad-free.

### Option 3: A Printed Welcome Book

Still works. Costs nothing. Nobody reads it, and you reprint it whenever the wifi password changes.

Worth naming because it is the honest baseline. If your place has one TV, low turnover, and a guest book that already does the job, you do not need any of this.

### Option 4: Casting or a Photo Slideshow

Some hosts load a slideshow of house information onto a USB stick or use the TV's built-in photo app.

It works exactly once. Every update means going to the property, and there is no way to greet a specific guest. Fine for a single unit you live near; unusable across several.

## What Works on Which Device

This is where most of the specific searches lead, so here is the direct answer per platform:

| Device | Works for a welcome screen? | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Fire TV Stick | Yes, best value | ~$30, install from the Amazon Appstore |
| Android TV / Google TV | Yes | Install from Google Play |
| Raspberry Pi | Yes | More control, ~$60 to $100 all in |
| Any TV with a browser | Yes | No install, open the web player |
| Roku | **No** | Closed platform, no signage app path |
| iPhone / iPad | Not as the screen | Your phone is the remote, not the display |

Two of those need their own explanation, because they are the most searched and the answers surprise people:

- **Roku:** Roku does not allow signage apps the way Fire TV and Android do. If your rental has a Roku TV, you will be adding a separate stick on another HDMI input. See [the honest Roku answer for hosts](/blog/airbnb-tv-app-roku).
- **iPhone:** There is no way to make an iPhone the TV screen, but there is an iOS app for managing your screens from your phone. See [what the iPhone actually does here](/blog/airbnb-tv-app-iphone).

## The Rule That Should Decide Your Choice

**Whatever you install must not take the TV hostage.**

A guest should be able to pick up the remote, press Home, and be in Netflix in two seconds. If your welcome screen makes that hard, you will read about it in a review, and "we couldn't figure out the TV" is a genuinely damaging sentence in a listing.

Before you commit to any app, test it yourself: install it, then try to leave it the way a tired guest would at midnight. On a Fire TV Stick, both Home and Back should get you out. On Android and Google TV, Home is the reliable exit.

## What a Good Welcome Screen Actually Shows

Keep it to what a guest needs in the first five minutes:

- **Welcome, [guest name]** — the single highest-impact detail
- **Wifi network and password**, in large type
- **Checkout time**, so nobody has to ask
- **Three or four local recommendations** you would send a friend to
- **How to reach you**, once, without being alarming

Resist filling it. The screen has one job, which is to answer the questions the guest is about to text you about.

## Setting It Up

For most hosts the whole job is about an hour, and most of that is deciding what to write:

1. Plug a streaming stick into the TV and, importantly, into a **wall outlet** rather than the TV's USB port, so it stays connected between guests
2. Install the signage app and open it
3. Enter the pairing code it shows into your dashboard
4. Fill in your wifi, checkout time and recommendations
5. Update the guest name between stays, from your phone

## FAQ

### Is there an official Airbnb TV app?

No. Airbnb publishes iPhone, Android and web apps for booking and managing stays. There is no Airbnb TV app. Hosts wanting a welcome screen use digital signage software instead.

### What is the best TV app for an Airbnb?

Whichever one runs on a cheap stick, updates from your phone, greets the guest by name, and lets guests exit to their own apps instantly. Ad-free signage runs about $10 per screen per month.

### Is there a free TV app for Airbnb hosts?

Free options are usually ad-supported, which means ads on the TV in your rental. Decide whether that fits your listing before choosing free over a small monthly fee.

### Will a welcome screen stop guests using the TV normally?

It should not. Test the exit yourself before a guest does.
