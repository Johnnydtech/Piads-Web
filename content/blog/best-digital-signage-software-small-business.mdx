## What the "starting price" hides

Per-screen list prices are only half the math for a small venue. The other half:

- **Hardware.** A platform that needs a $99 proprietary stick or a $65+ Raspberry Pi kit per screen costs more in year one than the subscription. PiAds and a few others run on the Fire TV Stick that's already plugged into millions of venue TVs.
- **Feature gates.** Several platforms put scheduling depth, reporting, or API access in higher tiers. The advertised price is rarely the price you end up on.
- **The earning line.** This one's binary: on every platform except PiAds, your screen is a pure expense. PiAds has a built-in local advertising marketplace — nearby businesses book slots on your screens, you approve every ad, and **you keep 75% of each booking**. One typical booking covers a screen's fee for months.

## 1. PiAds — best for local venues that want screens to pay for themselves

PiAds is digital signage built for cafes, gyms, salons, restaurants, and shops — with a twist no other tool has: a local ad marketplace on top of the CMS.

**What you get at $10/screen (no gates):** playlists with per-item timing, weekly scheduling with dayparts, media library, live screen health (online/offline, last-seen, last frame), guided four-step onboarding that ends with content actually playing, a built-in web player for testing without hardware, and native player apps for the entire Fire TV family — including the new Vega OS devices — plus Android, Raspberry Pi, and browsers.

**The earning part:** open the ad slots you choose to local businesses, approve every ad individually, keep 75% of the revenue. Screens with real foot traffic regularly net positive.

**Where others beat us — honestly:** Juuno's $5 tier is cheaper if you'll never run ads. OptiSigns and Yodeck have far bigger app libraries. ScreenCloud does corporate comms we don't. Raydiant will send technicians; we won't. And our ad marketplace is strongest for US local venues today.

## 2. Yodeck — best if you're happy maintaining Raspberry Pis

Mature platform, generous free single-screen plan, big app library. The trade: it's built around Raspberry Pi players you image and maintain (SD cards wear out; that's your job), and 2026 pricing now tiers from roughly $8 to $16 per screen with features spread across plans. Solid choice for the technically comfortable; overhead you didn't sign up for if you just want the menu on the TV. *Beats PiAds on:* app count, free entry plan, years at enterprise scale.

## 3. OptiSigns — best for feature breadth and kiosks

160+ apps, kiosk/interactive modes, AI add-ons. If you need niche integrations or touch screens, it's the strongest list here. The flip side is density: a lot of product (and tiering) between you and a menu board. *Beats PiAds on:* integrations, kiosk mode, enterprise options.

## 4. ScreenCloud — best for corporate employee comms

Polished, dashboard-on-screen features, SSO — priced for it at roughly $20+ per screen. For a customer-facing venue, you're paying enterprise rates for internal-comms features. *Beats PiAds on:* internal dashboards, corporate IT features, support depth.

## 5. Juuno — best minimal CMS at the lowest entry price

Clean, simple, $5/screen entry. Limits to know: reporting sits in the $9 tier, several features are still "coming soon," there's no screen-health view at all, and its hardware guide steers you toward buying its recommended $99 stick rather than the Fire TV hardware you may already own. *Beats PiAds on:* entry price, white-label reseller program.

## 6. Raydiant (Displai) — best for chains that want managed rollout

Demo-first sales, quote-based pricing, proprietary players, annual contracts — plus white-glove deployment that genuinely suits large franchises. Independent venues end up funding process and hardware they don't need. *Beats PiAds on:* managed installation, bundled employee-experience products.

## 7. Free options — fine for testing, costly in time

Free tiers (Yodeck's single screen, open-source players, spare-laptop-plus-browser setups) are real ways to experiment. The recurring costs are your hours and the missing pieces: scheduling depth, multi-screen management, health monitoring, support. Worth a weekend; rarely worth a year.

## FAQ

**What's the cheapest way to start digital signage?**
A TV you own + a Fire TV Stick you own + a free trial. With PiAds you can even skip hardware and test on the built-in web player in your browser.

**Do I need special signage hardware?**
No — that era is over for small venues. Fire TV Sticks, Android devices, Raspberry Pis, and browsers all work. Be wary of platforms that require their proprietary player.

**Can digital signage actually make money for my venue?**
On PiAds, yes: local advertisers book slots you approve and you keep 75%. On every other platform in this list, no — the screen is purely a cost.

**How long does switching platforms take?**
For a typical single venue: under an hour. Media re-uploads directly; playlists and schedules are recreated. Run both side by side for a week before cancelling anything.
