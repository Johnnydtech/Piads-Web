The cheapest signage player is the browser you already have. A spare laptop, an old desktop, a smart TV's built-in browser — point it at a web player, pair it, and you have a working screen with **zero new hardware**. Here's when that's genuinely enough, when it isn't, and the five-minute setup.

## When browser-based signage is enough

- **Testing a platform** before touching your TVs — this is the single best use; you see real content playing before spending a dollar
- **A screen driven by a PC anyway** — reception desks, back-office dashboards, a repurposed laptop
- **Temporary screens** — events, pop-ups, a weekend promo
- **Smart TVs with decent browsers** where installing apps isn't an option

## When to graduate to a stick

Browsers weren't built to run unattended for weeks. The known failure modes: the machine sleeps or updates overnight, the browser tab gets closed by a helpful employee, memory creep after days of video. If a screen matters every open hour, a **$30–60 Fire TV Stick** running a native player app is the reliability upgrade — it boots into signage, recovers from power cuts, and nobody can close the tab. The honest hierarchy for a small venue:

| Tier | Player | Best for |
|---|---|---|
| Free | Browser / spare laptop | Testing, temporary, PC-driven screens |
| $30–60 | Fire TV Stick (native app) | Every screen that matters daily |
| $80+ | Raspberry Pi | 24/7 duty, dual-screen, tinkerers |

## Browser signage survival settings

- **Disable sleep/hibernate** — the #1 cause of "the screen went black"
- **Turn off automatic restarts** where possible, or schedule them outside opening hours
- **Full-screen the tab** (F11) so no bookmarks bar joins your menu
- **Plug in ethernet** if the machine has a port; Wi-Fi dropouts are the #2 cause

## FAQ

**Is a web player really free?**
The player is — on PiAds the software is $0 for partner screens with approved ad slots. No hardware line, no software line.

**Does it keep playing offline?**
Cached content keeps playing through brief dropouts; a machine that reboots offline will need the connection back to resume. Native players (Fire TV, Android, Pi) handle offline more robustly.

**Can I run it on a smart TV browser?**
Often yes — results vary by TV. Test for an afternoon before trusting it; if the TV's browser sleeps or drops video, a stick fixes it permanently.

**Can I mix player types?**
Freely. Many venues run a stick on the customer-facing screen and the web player on a back-office monitor — one dashboard, same playlists.
