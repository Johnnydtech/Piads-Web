Fire TV and Android TV are the right platforms for a guest welcome screen, because both allow signage apps to be installed and to launch on their own. This is the practical setup guide.

## What You Need

| Item | Notes | Cost |
|---|---|---|
| The TV already in the rental | Any HDMI TV | $0 |
| Fire TV Stick, Android TV or Google TV box | Fire TV Stick is the value pick | ~$30 |
| A USB power adapter and wall outlet | Important, see below | ~$8 if not included |
| Signage software account | Ad-free from ~$10/screen/month | — |

Total for one room, if you already have the TV: about **$30 to $40 in hardware.**

## Step 1: Power It From the Wall, Not the TV

Do this first because it is the mistake that causes the most trouble later.

Most streaming sticks can draw power from the TV's USB port. It is tidy and it is wrong for a rental. On TV power the stick shuts down whenever the TV is off, which means:

- It misses updates you make between guests
- It shows as offline in your dashboard, so you cannot tell a sleeping stick from a broken one
- The guest waits while it boots and reconnects when they turn the TV on

On a wall outlet it stays connected all night and is already showing the right guest's name before anyone walks in.

## Step 2: Install the Player App

**On Fire TV:** From the home screen, search for the signage player by name and install it from the Amazon Appstore, the same as any other app.

**On Android TV or Google TV:** Install from Google Play.

Open the app once it is installed. It will show a **pairing code** on the screen, something like `ABC-DEF-12`.

## Step 3: Pair It to Your Account

On your phone or laptop, sign in to your signage dashboard, choose to connect a screen, and enter the code from the TV.

Give the screen a name you will recognize later. If you manage several properties, name it for the room and the property, not "Screen 1". You will thank yourself at ten units.

That is the pairing done. The TV is now yours to control remotely.

## Step 4: Build the Welcome Screen

Keep it to what a guest needs in the first five minutes:

- **Welcome, [guest name]** — the highest-impact line on the screen
- **Wifi network and password** in large, readable type
- **Checkout time**
- **Three or four local recommendations** you would actually send a friend to
- **How to reach you**, once

Resist adding more. The screen exists to answer the questions the guest is about to text you about, not to be a brochure.

## Step 5: Check the Exit Before a Guest Does

This is the step most hosts skip and the one that shows up in reviews.

Pick up the remote and leave the app the way a tired guest would at midnight:

- **On Fire TV:** both the **Home** button and the **Back** button should get you out to the normal TV interface.
- **On Android TV and Google TV:** use **Home**. Note that on the Android build the Back button is deliberately blocked, so Home is the reliable exit. Test it and, if you mention the screen in your check-in message, tell guests to press Home.

If anything you install prevents a guest reaching Netflix in a couple of seconds, remove it. "We couldn't work out the TV" is a genuinely costly sentence in a listing review.

## Step 6: Update Between Guests

The whole point of doing this rather than printing a card is that the next change costs you ten seconds from your phone.

Between stays, change the guest name. When the router password changes, update it once. When a recommendation closes, replace it while you remember.

## Multiple Properties

The setup above is per screen, but the management is not. Once several rooms are paired you can update from one dashboard, which is where this starts saving real time.

Two things worth doing at scale:

**Turn off offline alerts on TVs that guests switch off.** A rental TV that goes dark overnight is normal, not a fault. Alerting on it trains you to ignore the notifications that actually matter, so silence the screens that are supposed to go dark and keep alerts on the ones that should stay lit.

**Standardize the hardware.** Buying the same stick for every unit means one set of instructions for your cleaner and one troubleshooting path for you.

## FAQ

### Is there an Airbnb TV app for Android?

No. Airbnb's Android app manages stays. Hosts install digital signage software on Android TV, Google TV or Fire TV to create a welcome screen.

### How do I set up a welcome screen on a Fire TV Stick?

Power it from the wall, connect wifi, install the player from the Amazon Appstore, open it, and enter the pairing code in your dashboard. About fifteen minutes.

### Should I power the streaming stick from the TV's USB port?

No. Use a wall outlet so it stays online and current between guests.
