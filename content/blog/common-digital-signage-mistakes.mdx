Most digital signage does not fail because of the technology. It fails because of a handful of avoidable decisions, usually made in the first week and never revisited.

Here are the mistakes that show up again and again, and the specific fix for each.

## 1. Too Much Text

The single most common mistake. Someone builds a slide in the same mindset as a flyer, and packs in a paragraph.

Nobody reads a paragraph on a wall. A viewer gives your screen about three seconds while they wait for coffee.

**The fix:** Six words or fewer in the headline. Under 15 words on the slide. If you need more, you need a second slide, or a QR code that moves the detail to their phone.

## 2. Mounting the Screen Too High

Screens get mounted at architectural height, above a doorway or near the ceiling, because it looks tidy. At that height customers crane their necks and stop looking after a few seconds.

**The fix:** Eye level, within about 15 degrees. Roughly 60 to 65 inches to screen center for standing viewers, 50 to 60 inches for seated. Mount lower than looks right. See [screen placement best practices](/blog/screen-placement-best-practices).

## 3. Stale Content

A screen promoting an event that ended two months ago does real damage. It reads as neglect, and customers generalize from it.

**The fix:** One named owner, a recurring calendar block, and end dates on every promotion so it removes itself when it expires.

## 4. The Wrong Screen for the Light

A consumer TV in a sun-facing window is a black mirror by 2pm. No content strategy survives that.

**The fix:** Survey the light before you buy. Direct sun needs a commercial display at 2,500 nits or higher. Otherwise, move the screen out of the glare.

## 5. No Call to Action

Beautiful photography, brand colors, no ask. The customer feels vaguely warm about you and does nothing.

**The fix:** Every loop should contain at least one clear action. Order this. Scan this. Ask about that. Join here.

## 6. The Loop Is Too Long

A five-minute loop means a customer waiting two minutes sees a third of your message and never the part you cared about most.

**The fix:** Match loop length to dwell time. Under 60 seconds for quick-service. Two to three minutes where people wait longer. Put your most important slide early and repeat it.

## 7. Treating the TV Like a Website

Small fonts, dense tables, multi-column layouts, tiny logos in the corner. Designs that work at arm's length on a laptop fail at 12 feet.

**The fix:** Design at actual viewing distance. Preview it from where customers will really stand, not from your desk chair.

## 8. Ignoring Dwell Time

Screens get placed where there is wall space, not where there is attention. A screen in a corridor people walk briskly past will underperform the same screen beside the queue.

**The fix:** Put screens where people are already stationary and mildly bored. Queues, waiting areas, seating.

## 9. No Monitoring

A screen goes dark on a Friday. Nobody notices until Tuesday. The venue paid for four days of blank wall and looked broken to every customer.

**The fix:** Use software that alerts you when a screen stops checking in. Then actually keep those alerts useful: mute the screens that are supposed to go dark, like a guest TV switched off overnight, so you do not train yourself to ignore the emails that matter.

## 10. Powering the Stick from the TV's USB Port

A convenient mistake with a slow-burning cost. A streaming stick on TV power shuts down whenever the TV does, so it misses updates and shows up as offline constantly.

**The fix:** Wall outlet. It costs a few dollars and the player stays connected overnight, ready before you open.

## 11. Vertical Content on a Horizontal Screen

Someone sends over an Instagram story graphic and it goes up with black bars on both sides, using half the screen.

**The fix:** Design for the orientation you actually mounted. If you want portrait content, rotate the screen and set the orientation in your software.

## 12. Never Measuring Anything

Most venues never learn whether the screen did anything, so the budget conversation next year is pure opinion.

**The fix:** Pick one measurable thing per promotion. A QR scan count, a promo code, a menu item's sales before and after it went on screen. See [how to measure local advertising ROI](/blog/how-to-measure-local-advertising-roi).

## The Pattern Behind All of Them

Nearly every mistake here comes from designing for yourself instead of the person standing 12 feet away with three seconds of attention and no reason to care yet.

Before publishing anything, stand where your customer stands, at the distance they stand, for the time they actually have. Most bad slides fail that test instantly.

## FAQ

### What are common digital signage mistakes?

Too much text, mounting too high, stale content, the wrong screen for the light, no call to action, an overlong loop, web-style design, ignoring dwell time, no monitoring, powering the player from the TV, wrong orientation, and never measuring results.

### How much text should be on a digital signage slide?

Six words or fewer in the headline, under 15 on the slide. Move detail to a QR code.

### How long should a digital signage loop be?

Under 60 seconds for most venues, up to two or three minutes where waits are long.
