Indoor golf simulator lounges have gone from a niche hobby to one of the fastest-growing local entertainment categories, and most of them are already covered in screens — bay monitors showing shot tracer data, a TV over the bar, maybe a lobby screen nobody's touched since install day. The simulator screens are doing their job. Everything else is usually idle, or stuck looping a cable channel that has nothing to do with the venue.

That's a waste, because a golf simulator lounge has one of the best attention profiles of any local venue: groups book bays for an hour or two, order food and drinks while they play, and spend a meaningful chunk of that time not actually swinging — waiting for their turn in the group, reviewing shot data, or hanging out at the bar between rounds.

## Why golf simulator venues and driving ranges are a strong fit for digital signage

**Long bookings, built-in downtime.** A typical bay reservation runs 60-90 minutes for a group of 2-4. Within that window, any one player is actively hitting for a fraction of the time — the rest is watching, waiting, and talking. That's attention a non-simulator screen can capture without competing with the game itself.

**A built-in reason to look up.** Bay screens already hold attention for shot tracers and stats. A nearby screen that extends that same moment — leaderboard standings, a league schedule, today's bar specials — gets seen because guests are already primed to look at a screen, not away from one.

**Groups with disposable income.** Golf simulator lounges and modern driving ranges skew toward an adult demographic with money to spend on equipment, lessons, and food and drink while they're there. That's a profile local advertisers pay to reach.

**Natural crossover with food, drink, and golf retail.** Most venues run a bar or kitchen alongside the bays, and nearly every guest is a golfer with gear, apparel, and lesson needs. That overlap is exactly what turns an idle screen into ad revenue instead of just a cost.

## Content ideas by screen location

| Screen location | What to show | Why it works |
|---|---|---|
| Bay monitors | Shot tracer, stats, group's current game | Already the focus of attention — leave this one alone |
| Bay surround / adjacent screen | Leaderboard, league standings, next-group queue | Seen during the natural downtime between swings |
| Front desk / check-in | Bay wait times, open bay availability, lesson booking | Answers the first question every walk-in has |
| Bar area | Drink specials, happy hour, food menu | Captures the "what should we order" moment between holes |
| Lobby / waiting area | League sign-ups, upcoming tournaments, local ads | Fills dead time for groups waiting on a bay |
| Pro shop | Club and gear spotlights, lesson packages | Purchase decision point for equipment and instruction |

## Six ways simulator lounges and ranges actually use their screens

**1. Turn bay downtime into a leaderboard rotation.** A screen next to (not on top of) the simulator display can rotate through today's long-drive leaderboard, league standings, or a tournament bracket. It doesn't compete with the game — it fills the gaps between shots with something guests actually want to check.

**2. Cut down on front-desk questions.** Bay wait times and open availability on a lobby screen answer the five questions every front desk fields all day, freeing staff to handle bookings and lessons instead.

**3. Sell the bar harder, automatically.** A screen near the bar rotating through happy hour windows, a food combo, or a seasonal cocktail converts better than a laminated menu nobody reads twice. Groups playing for 90 minutes will order more than once if you remind them.

**4. Fill leagues and tournaments.** A sign-up flyer taped to a wall gets ignored. A screen showing live league standings next to sign-up dates reaches the competitive golfers already in the building, mid-season, when they're most likely to commit.

## The honest trade-off

If you're running one or two bays and a single TV over the bar handles everything fine, you probably don't need a signage platform yet — a chalkboard special and a printed league schedule work at that scale. Digital signage earns its keep once you've got multiple screens to coordinate (bay surrounds, bar, lobby, pro shop), content that needs to change by time of day or by league night, or an interest in turning screen time you already paid for into ad income. Don't overbuild a single static display.

**Do I need to replace my simulator software or bay monitors?**
No. PiAds runs on the screens around your bays — lobby, bar, pro shop — not the simulator display itself. Most venues keep their simulator software untouched and add PiAds to everything else.

**Can I keep ads off my screens and just use it for content?**
Yes. Ad slots are entirely optional and every ad needs your approval — plenty of venues run PiAds purely for leaderboards, menus, and league schedules with zero advertising, and turn ad slots on later with no setup changes.

**What kind of local businesses actually want to advertise in a golf simulator lounge?**
Golf retailers and club fitters, sports bars and restaurants, chiropractors and sports medicine clinics, and local breweries are the most common advertisers, since they're chasing the same adult, disposable-income audience already booking your bays.

**How many screens does a typical simulator lounge run?**
A small lounge with 3-4 bays might run 4-6 screens (bay surrounds, bar, lobby, pro shop); a larger facility with leagues and a full bar can run a dozen or more. At $10/screen/month, cost scales with exactly what you add.

**Does this work for outdoor driving ranges too?**
Yes — the same approach applies to range buildings, hitting bay overhangs, and clubhouse screens at traditional driving ranges. Leaderboards, bucket pricing, lesson promos, and local ads carry over directly, as long as the screen itself is indoors or weatherproofed.
