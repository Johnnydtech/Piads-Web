If your rental has Roku TVs, this is the article you actually need, and it starts with a disappointing sentence.

**There is no Airbnb TV app for Roku, and there is no digital signage app for Roku either.** Not from us, not from our competitors. This is not a gap someone will fill next quarter; it is a consequence of how Roku is built.

Here is why, and what to do instead.

> PiAds is not affiliated with, endorsed by, or sponsored by Airbnb or Roku.

## Why Roku Is Different

Fire TV and Android TV are Android underneath. You can sideload or publish a signage app, it can run a web view, and it can launch itself when the device wakes. That is why nearly every signage platform supports them.

Roku is a closed system, and three things block signage specifically:

**1. No general web view.** Roku channels are written in BrightScript with SceneGraph for layout. There is no browser component to host a player in. A Roku version would be a full rewrite in a proprietary language, not a port.

**2. No auto-launch.** This is the one that ends the conversation. On consumer Roku hardware, a third-party channel cannot start itself when the TV powers on, and it cannot replace the home screen. Your guest would have to find your channel and open it deliberately.

A welcome screen a guest has to go looking for is not a welcome screen. The entire value is that it is already there when they walk in and turn on the TV.

**3. Certification.** The Roku channel store is built for consumer entertainment channels, not business signage. Even a well-built channel faces a review process aimed at a different kind of product.

Anyone promising you a Roku signage app should be asked directly whether it auto-launches. If it does not, it does not solve the problem you have.

## What Roku Does Give Hosts: Guest Mode

Credit where it is due. Roku's **Guest Mode** is the single best hosting feature on any TV platform, and many hosts choose Roku specifically for it.

Guest Mode lets a guest sign into their own Netflix, Hulu or Disney+ accounts, then **automatically signs them out on the checkout date you set.** That solves a real and irritating problem: ghost accounts left logged in on your TVs, and the awkward day a guest finds someone else's profile still there.

If your rental is on Roku, turn Guest Mode on. It is the reason to keep those TVs.

Hosts do report occasional trouble with it, usually around the checkout date field erroring out on one TV in a fleet. It is generally reliable, but check it during your turnover rather than assuming.

## Your Three Real Options

### Option 1: Roku Guest Mode Plus a Printed Card

Use Guest Mode for the account hygiene, and keep the wifi password and checkout time on a well-designed card by the TV or on the fridge.

**Cost:** Nothing.
**Trade-off:** No remote updates, no guest name, and you are back to reprinting when the wifi changes. For a single unit with low turnover this is genuinely fine.

### Option 2: Add a Streaming Stick on Another Input

Put a $30 Fire TV Stick on HDMI 2 and run your welcome screen there.

**Cost:** About $30 per TV, plus signage software.
**Trade-off:** The guest has to switch inputs to see it, which means most of them will not. You also lose the auto-launch benefit, because the TV wakes on whatever input it was last on.

Worth doing if you want remote-updatable house information and are willing to point at it in your check-in message. Not worth doing if you expected the screen to greet people on its own.

### Option 3: Swap the TV or the Stick

If you are furnishing a new unit, or replacing a TV anyway, choose a Fire TV or Google TV model instead of Roku. Then the welcome screen works the way it is supposed to, and you can still get most of the account-hygiene benefit by signing guests out manually or using profiles.

**Cost:** Nothing extra if you were buying a TV anyway.
**Trade-off:** You give up Guest Mode's automatic checkout-date logout, which is a real loss.

## The Honest Trade

For hosts, Roku and welcome screens are close to mutually exclusive today, and both sides have something worth having:

| | Roku | Fire TV / Google TV |
|---|---|---|
| Automatic guest logout | **Yes**, Guest Mode | No, manual |
| Welcome screen that auto-launches | **No** | Yes |
| Remote-updatable house info | No | Yes |
| Cost | You likely own it | ~$30 per TV |

If your biggest pain is ghost streaming accounts, stay on Roku. If your biggest pain is answering the same wifi question at midnight, you want a Fire TV Stick.

## What We Would Tell You Not To Do

Do not buy a signage subscription expecting it to work on your Roku TVs. It will not, and no amount of setup will change that. We would rather tell you now than take the money.

If Roku ever opens a path for auto-launching business channels, this changes. Until then, the truthful answer is that Roku is the wrong TV for a guest welcome screen and the right TV for guest account management.

## FAQ

### Is there an Airbnb TV app for Roku?

No. Airbnb publishes no TV app on any platform, and Roku does not permit digital signage apps that auto-launch.

### Why can't digital signage run on Roku?

No general web view, no auto-launch for third-party channels, and a channel store built for entertainment rather than business signage.

### What should an Airbnb host with Roku TVs do?

Turn on Guest Mode for automatic checkout-date logouts, and either keep house information on a printed card or add a $30 stick on a second input.
