Canva is where most small venues already design their menus and promos — the only missing piece is getting that design onto the TV behind the counter, reliably, without a laptop dangling from an HDMI cable. Here's the clean way.

## The short version

1. Design in Canva at **1920 × 1080 px** (landscape TV) or 1080 × 1920 (portrait)
2. **Export as PNG** (best quality for menus) or MP4 for animated designs
3. Upload to your signage dashboard, drop it into a playlist
4. The TV updates within seconds — and re-updates every time you re-export

No HDMI laptops, no USB sticks walked to the TV, no smart-TV photo apps that sleep after an hour.

## Step 1 — start from the right canvas

In Canva, choose **Custom size → 1920 × 1080 px**. This matches the TV exactly, so your design fills the screen edge to edge with no black bars and no cropping surprises. (Canva's "Presentation" size is also 1920×1080 and works fine.)

For vertical screens — portrait menu boards, window displays — use **1080 × 1920** and set the screen to portrait in your signage dashboard.

TV design tips that save re-dos:

- **Bigger text than feels natural** — a menu is read from 6–15 feet away; body text below ~40px disappears
- **Keep a margin** — some TVs overscan slightly; keep critical text 5% away from edges
- **High contrast** — dark text on light or the reverse; subtle grays vanish on cheap panels

## Step 2 — export

- **Menus, price lists, promos:** Share → Download → **PNG** (crisper text than JPG)
- **Animated designs:** Download → **MP4 video** — plays as a video slide in your playlist

## FAQ

**Does my Canva design update on the TV automatically when I edit it?**
Not by itself — Canva is the design tool, not the delivery. Re-export and re-upload (a minute, total). The upside of this decoupling: your screen never breaks because a Canva embed hiccuped.

**PNG or JPG?**
PNG for anything with text — menu boards especially. JPG compression makes small text fuzzy on a big panel.

**Can I show a multi-page Canva design?**
Yes — export each page as its own PNG and add them all to the playlist; each page gets its own on-screen duration.

**What about Canva animations?**
Export as MP4 and upload as a video. Keep loops short (10–20s) so the board doesn't feel repetitive.
