A medical spa sells results it can't fully show in a five-minute consultation. Botox, laser, microneedling, and body contouring are all services clients need to *see* and trust before they book — and a waiting room full of outdated tri-fold brochures doesn't build that trust. A screen showing real before/after results, current promotions, and treatment explainers does.

Medspas also sit in a strange middle zone: part medical office, part day spa, part retail counter selling skincare product. That mix means the signage playbook that works for a dermatology clinic or a day spa doesn't map cleanly onto a medspa. Here's what actually works, room by room.

## Why Medspas Are a Natural Fit for Digital Signage

Three things make medical spas unusually well-suited to screens:

- **High-consideration purchases.** A $600 laser package or a membership plan needs more explanation than a price list can give. Video and before/after visuals do real selling work while a client waits.
- **Long dwell times.** Consultations, numbing time, and recovery periods mean clients sit for 15–45 minutes with nothing to do. That's premium attention most venues would pay for.
- **A retail component.** Most medspas sell skincare lines at the front desk. A screen near that display converts browsing into add-on sales the same way a grocery endcap does.

## Where to Place Screens: A Room-by-Room Guide

| Room | Primary content | Dwell time | Screen priority |
|---|---|---|---|
| Reception / waiting area | Service menu, promotions, before/after galleries, local ads | 10–20 min | High — do this first |
| Consultation room | Treatment explainers, pricing, financing options | 15–30 min | Medium |
| Treatment / procedure room | Calming visuals, brand content (often muted) | 20–60 min | Low — nice-to-have |
| Retail / product wall | Skincare line features, bundle offers | 2–5 min | Medium |

The honest trade-off: a single-location medspa with one or two treatment rooms gets most of the value from one well-placed reception screen. Clients spend their most "open to persuasion" minutes there, before a consultation, not during a procedure where they're often lying down with their eyes closed. Don't buy a screen for every room on day one — start at reception, add a retail-wall screen once that one is earning its keep, and only wire up treatment rooms if client feedback specifically asks for it.

## Content That Actually Works

**Before/after galleries.** This is the highest-converting content type for aesthetic services — but it comes with a compliance requirement most generic signage advice skips: **you need documented client consent before displaying any before/after photo**, even blurred or cropped. Keep signed releases on file and only load pre-cleared images into your playlist. Most medspas build a small rotating set of 8–12 approved pairs rather than trying to showcase every result.

**Treatment explainers.** A 30–60 second video or slide sequence explaining what a treatment actually involves reduces consultation time and pre-qualifies clients who weren't a fit anyway.

**Financing and package pricing.** Aesthetic treatments are often sold in packages (6 sessions, membership tiers). A clear pricing slide in the waiting area primes that conversation before the consultation even starts.

**Seasonal promotions.** Dayparting and scheduled playlists let you run "wedding season" or "holiday party" promotions automatically without re-uploading content every few weeks — set it once and let it rotate on schedule.

**Retail cross-sell.** If you sell a skincare line, show it. Clients who just had a facial are primed to buy the product that supports it.

## FAQ

**Do medspas need a screen in every treatment room?**
No. Start with reception, where dwell time and attention are highest. Treatment-room screens are a nice-to-have, not a priority — many medspas never add them.

**Can I show before/after photos on digital signage?**
Yes, but only with documented client consent for each image. Keep signed releases on file and only load pre-approved photos into your rotation.

**What's the cheapest way to test digital signage before buying hardware?**
A browser-based player on a spare laptop or tablet costs nothing and lets you run a reception screen before committing to a Fire TV Stick.
